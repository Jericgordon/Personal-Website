My AT map

Part 1

In 2022 I hiked the 2193.7 miles of the Appalachian trail, spanning from Georgia to Maine. It was 6 months of my life, and still one of the coolest experiences I've ever had, and I tried to take at least one picture a day.

Fast forward to 2025, I'm in grad school for computer science, and in web development. My approach to projects in grad school has always to do something cool within the constraints of the rubric, but when push comes to shove, making something cool always comes first.

I've had the photos from my through hike as a rotating desktop background for years at this point, but I've always wanted them to be more accessible. Before this project, showing people my photos always involved sitting down with them and scrolling through my Google Photos. While this worked, this wasn't sharable to a larger audience.

I'd seen news articles do effect that showed where an event happened as you scrolled past. I pictured doing something like that for my pictures, with a line moving slowly along the trail.

After looking through, I found this map that I really liked, and conveniently, it was already an SVG. The challenge then was highlighting the pictures segment we were currently on. To accomplish this, I manually drew and smoothed a line as an SVG object that followed the trail. Then, I used the inbuilt property of SVG lines which allows you to put dashes on the line. Except, in my case, I made the dashes spaced twice the length of the whole line, and moved the offset as I scrolled. This allowed me to have the effect I wanted of a the line being filled in as the user scrolled down, and the map moved up.

I made the map take up about half of the screen, and used Bootstrap cards to host the pictures as well as some text describing what happened there. While this version isn't hosted anymore, you can see a video of the product here. While this isn't the version I settled on, I am really happy with how much of it turned out.
