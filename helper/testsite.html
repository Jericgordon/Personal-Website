Appalachian Trail Map

Part 2 - The Good and Bad

There were a lot of things I liked about my first version of an AT map.

  • The visual of the line scrolling up was really cool
  • The inverse scrolling effect allowed the map to start in Georgia (where I started)
  • It was nice to be able to write captions for the photos

However, there were also several considerable drawbacks.

  • The map absolutely did not work on mobile
  • The scrolling effect was slightly jittery, especially if you scrolled fast
  • Captions meant that it was hard to make pictures dense. I didn't allow me to put all the pictures I wanted on the trail
  • My picture / story density wasn't evenly spread throughout the trail

I'll touch on all of the points briefly. The importance of mobile accessibility became increasingly apparent to me. I would text the link to friends with the instruction "Don't open this on mobile." That's not good design.

The scrolling effect was also very problematic. In hindsight, the issues with jittering, especially if scrolling quickly, were the nail in the coffin. I made the jitter better by limiting the number of times the position could update in a second. (This may be counter-intuative, but part of what was slowing it down was the calculation of old positions of the map, and unnecessary re-rendering). And Looking at the news articles that had inspired this, they had the same issues I did. Fixing this would be a major job.

Even if I did fix this, I'd have to find a way to make the scrolling effect change speeds throughout, as pictures were already not lining up a their intended place. And there wasn't clear indication in the previous version >where the intended place was>. It was more of a gesture towards the approximate location.

I had been able to get away with it in the previous edition by trying to equally space pictures, but my best pictures were all up north anyway. All of this led me to the conclusion that these problems were big enough that changing the approach might be warranted.

I'll discuss my new Approach in the next post!
